## What this phase is

This phase builds the machinery for the day something goes wrong: the incident response program under **§164.308(a)(6)**, and the breach notification obligations under **45 CFR §164.400–414**. It publishes the IR plan and severity model, the detection and triage process, containment and recovery procedures, the ransomware playbook, the statutory four-factor breach risk assessment, the full notification requirements, and the notification templates — then tests all of it in a tabletop exercise.

## The distinction that governs everything here

A **security incident** (§164.304) is not the same thing as a **breach** (§164.402). Most incidents are not breaches. But the analysis runs the other way from how people expect:

> A breach is **presumed** unless the covered entity **demonstrates a low probability of compromise** through the four-factor assessment. Under §164.414(b) the burden of proof sits with MercyBridge — not with a regulator to prove harm.

Two consequences follow. First, **encryption is decisive**: properly encrypted ePHI is not "unsecured PHI," so its loss is not a breach at all — which is what the Phase 05 work bought. Second, **paper can never be safe-harboured**, which is exactly what determined one of the three incidents below.

## The three incidents

| Incident | Individuals | Why it was not a breach |
|---|---|---|
| INC-2026-0031 — lost laptop and paper list | 31 | The laptop was encrypted and safe-harboured. **The printed clinic list was not** — so the determination turned entirely on the paper, which was recovered intact within 26 hours |
| INC-2026-0044 — business email compromise | 3,318 | Factor 3 was decisive: forensics showed no bulk export, no search activity, no forwarding rule. The actor was pursuing payment redirection |
| INC-2026-0058 — business associate SFTP misconfiguration | 612 | Associate access logs, obtained inside the contractual 5-day window, showed no retrieval. MercyBridge reached its **own** determination rather than accepting the associate's |

## What the tabletop actually proved

TTX-2026-01 produced a **SEV-1 declaration in nine minutes**, dual activation of Hospital Incident Command alongside the CSIRT, and a recorded exercise of the **CMIO's clinical containment veto** — because isolating a clinical system can itself stop care.

Most usefully, the four-factor assessment run under time pressure **correctly failed to rebut the presumption**. Where an actor has exfiltrated data, a low-probability finding is not available, and the exercise confirmed the team would not talk itself into one.

It also surfaced three significant findings that remain open, and it **disproved an assumption**: downtime reconciliation ran slower than the clinical target, so **R-43 was deliberately held rather than marked as reduced**.
