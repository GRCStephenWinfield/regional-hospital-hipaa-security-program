## What this phase is

Phases 01 through 07 were MercyBridge telling itself how good its program was. This phase is where somebody else checks. Five independent lenses are pointed at the same program — a penetration test, a vulnerability assessment, an internal audit, a HITRUST CSF validated assessment, and a self-run OCR Audit Protocol readiness review — and the phase is judged on what they found, not on what the program claimed.

The Security Rule does not name any of these instruments. **§164.308(a)(8) evaluation** requires periodic technical and non-technical evaluation, and OCR's enforcement record makes clear that an evaluation performed only by the people who built the controls is worth less than one performed by people with no stake in the answer. Everything here is that principle applied.

## The result that matters most

> **Two risks went up.**

A favourable assessment cycle that produces only reductions is a cycle that was not really testing anything. Independent testing disproved two things MercyBridge believed:

- **R-41 raised, Low → Moderate.** PT-03 found an internet-facing appointment application, live since 2018 and superseded in 2023, holding **6,880 records** — in no ePHI inventory, with no owner, no logging and no patching path. The Low residual had rested on the assumption that discovery scanning had bounded the sprawl. It had not. The application is gone; the *score* still had to move, because the score expressed a belief and the belief was wrong.
- **R-23 raised, 8 → 10.** Phase 07 reduced enterprise ransomware **conditionally**, in writing, on a joint restoration test with the EHR vendor in 2026-09. The test did not fail — **it did not happen**. The condition lapsed exactly as written, so the residual reverted. This is why conditional reductions get written down with their reversion rule attached.

**ADR-0021** records the standing rule: where independent testing disproves an assumption, raise or restore the affected rating rather than only closing the finding.

## The five lenses, and where they converged

The value is not in any single report. It is in the **overlap**: where two or more independent lenses, working separately, land on the same defect.

| Converged finding | Lenses that found it | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| Boundary between workstation and legacy device VLANs not what the documentation said | Pen test (PT-01) · HITRUST | R-10, R-17 — IV-06, IV-07 |
| Vendor remote-support credentials under-governed | Pen test (PT-02) · Internal audit · HITRUST | R-02, R-06 — CAP-02 |
| Enterprise-scale restoration never validated | Internal audit · HITRUST (CAP-01 at **25%**, the lowest score in the assessment) · OCR readiness | R-23, R-47 — **M-2 overdue** |
| Physical consistency at ambulatory sites | **Three** lenses | CAP-08 / IA-F-04 |
| Asset inventory completeness | Pen test (PT-03) · HITRUST | **Raises R-41** |

Three of the five converged items are the *same* item wearing different clothes: **recoverability is not proven at scale.** That is the honest headline of the phase, and it is what Phase 09 inherits.

## Why i1 and not r2

HITRUST **i1** — 182 requirement statements, threat-adaptive, one-year certification — was selected over **r2** deliberately. r2 is the deeper certification, but it assesses maturity across five levels and a program whose controls have been operating for months rather than years cannot evidence *Measured* and *Managed* maturity honestly. Choosing i1 buys a real, externally validated certification now, and establishes the operating history that makes an r2 credible later. **ADR-0020** carries the reasoning and the r2 decision point.
