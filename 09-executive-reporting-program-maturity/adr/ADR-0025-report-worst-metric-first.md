# ADR-0025 — Report the Worst Metric First

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | **Accepted** |
| Date | 2026-12-02 |
| Deciders | Daniel Cho (CISO), Karen Boyd (CCO); endorsed by the Board 2026-12-18 |

## Context

The December pack had a genuinely good story to tell: a certification, a Satisfactory audit opinion, KEV instances at zero, no reportable breaches. It also had four red measures on the Board scorecard — nine across the full 36-metric catalogue — an overdue item, and two risks that went up.

Conventional practice puts the achievements first and the exceptions in an appendix. That format optimises for how the report *feels* rather than for what the Board *does* with it.

## Decision

The Board and Committee pack **opens** with the exceptions: red metrics, overdue items, risks raised, and items outside appetite. Achievements follow. This is the standing format, not a one-off gesture in a good year.

## Rationale

Three arguments, in order of weight.

**A Board that reads good news first learns to skim.** Attention is finite and front-loaded. Whatever occupies the first page is what gets the scrutiny, so the first page should hold the things that most need a decision.

**It makes the good news credible.** A pack that reports 74% detection while omitting that the first measurement was 48% invites the question of what else was omitted. Reporting the bad number first is what makes the good number believable — this is the same principle that governs risk ratings under ADR-0021.

**It protects the reporter.** Once the format is fixed, presenting an uncomfortable number is compliance with a standard rather than an act of individual courage. The format does the work so no individual has to.

## Consequences

- A director recorded at the December meeting that this was the first management pack in her tenure to lead with its worst number, and the Board **adopted the format as standing**.
- The format will be at its hardest in a bad year, which is the year it exists for.
- Combined with ADR-0023, the disclosure is not discretionary: items reach the agenda by rule and appear at the front by format.
