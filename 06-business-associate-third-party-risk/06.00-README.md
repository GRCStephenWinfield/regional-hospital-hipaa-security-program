## What this phase is

This is the phase with **no equivalent in banking, manufacturing, or federal compliance work**. HIPAA extends the covered entity's obligations down a contractual chain: an organization that handles ePHI on MercyBridge's behalf is a **business associate**, its subcontractors are business associates in their own right, and under HITECH they carry **direct liability** to OCR.

But the covered entity's liability does not transfer. MercyBridge answers to OCR for ePHI held by ~180 outside organizations it does not control. OCR has brought enforcement actions on the **simple absence of a signed agreement** — no exposure of data required. This phase builds the program that makes that exposure governable: identification, tiering, agreements, due diligence, the downstream chain, cloud providers, monitoring, incident coordination, and termination.

## The concentration problem

The single largest exposure in the whole register is **R-28**: the entire **2.1-million-record clinical archive** sits with one business associate, the vendor-hosted Halcyon EHR. Replacing a core EHR is a multi-year undertaking, so the concentration cannot be engineered away in this cycle.

The tempting answer is to accept it and move on. This phase rejects that. A **nine-measure treatment plan** — independent ePHI export outside the vendor's control, a tested exit path, enhanced contractual terms, assurance beyond reading a SOC 2, a joint incident exercise, key-custody escalation, SLA and recovery commitments, downstream mapping, and an executive escalation route — takes **R-28 from High (15) to Moderate (10)**. Impact stays floored at 5, because losing the clinical archive is catastrophic no matter what controls exist; the entire reduction comes from likelihood.

That closes the last High risk in the program.
