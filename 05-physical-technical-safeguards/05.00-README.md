## What this phase is

This phase implements the two remaining safeguard families: **§164.310 Physical Safeguards** (facility access, workstation use, workstation security, device and media controls) and **§164.312 Technical Safeguards** (access control, audit controls, integrity, person or entity authentication, transmission security). It also delivers **POL-16 … POL-24**, completing the 24-policy suite.

Where Phase 04 governed people and process, this phase is where the technology actually closes risk — and where the hospital context bites hardest. Every control here had to survive a simple test: **does it still work at three in the morning in a resuscitation bay?**

## The clinical-usability principle

A safeguard that slows a clinician in an emergency is not a safe safeguard. So the design deliberately bends to the setting:

- **Automatic logoff graded by care area** — 20 minutes in ED resuscitation, 5 minutes in exam rooms and virtual desktops — co-signed by the CISO and CMIO rather than a single enterprise interval
- **Badge tap plus PIN** on 3,550 endpoints, because a phone-based factor is unusable in a sterile field
- **Break-the-glass emergency access** available to every clinician with no pre-approval — the compensating control is **100% retrospective review**, not prevention
- **Segmentation instead of patching** for 1,300 devices the manufacturer no longer supports, because removing them from service is not clinically an option

| HIPAA policy suite | 15 of 24 | **24 of 24 complete** |
| Residual posture (all 56 risks) | 11 High | **1 High · 20 Moderate · 35 Low** |

All **six High risks** handed over from Phase 04 (R-01, R-09, R-11, R-17, R-24, R-40) close here. The single remaining High depends on business-associate assurance and carries to Phase 06.

## Why encryption mattered most

ePHI encrypted to HHS guidance is **not "unsecured PHI"** — so its loss does not trigger breach notification. Taking encryption at rest from 51 to 68 of 68 systems didn't just reduce a risk score; it moved the entire estate under the **breach safe harbor**. That single fact carries most of the business case, and it is also what the 2025 NPRM would make mandatory.
